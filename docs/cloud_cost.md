# Cloud-cost model

Geometry-aware routing is a cost question, not just a fidelity question:
on time-billed quantum cloud, deeper circuits bill more. This page turns
the measured two-qubit depths into relative QPU cost. Generated by
`scripts/cloud_cost.py`; numbers come from `results/tables/summary.csv`
and `results/tables/scaling.csv`.

## Pricing

- **IBM Quantum Pay-As-You-Go list price:** $1.60 per QPU second, as
  reported by a third-party tracker (quantumcomputingcost.com). This was
  not verified on IBM's own pricing page — re-check there before citing.
- **AWS Braket:** $0.30 per task + per-shot device pricing (e.g. Rigetti
  Cepheus $0.000425/shot, IonQ Forte $0.08/shot). Source:
  aws.amazon.com/braket/pricing.

## Model and assumptions

1. On time-billed QPUs, execution time per shot = fixed overhead
   (readout, reset, repetition delay) + depth-proportional gate time.
2. Relative cost per shot = depth_A / depth_B, same device and shot
   count. This ratio is an **upper bound**, exact only if per-shot time
   were purely depth-proportional (zero fixed overhead). It needs no
   assumed gate time.
3. Protected runs discard shots, and discarded shots still bill, so cost
   per *useful* shot is multiplied by 1 / yield.
4. On per-shot billing (Braket), depth does not change the bill. This
   model applies to time billing only.

## Observed billing

Our own hardware run tells against the pure-depth picture: the
`ibm_fez` job page showed **"Total usage 4 s"** for 3 circuits × 2000
shots = 6000 shots, i.e. about **0.67 ms per shot** — hundreds of times
more than any plausible two-qubit gate time. Per-shot time is dominated
by fixed overhead, so real relative savings from shallower circuits are
much smaller than the depth ratio. Screenshot:
`frontend/public/images/ibm_fez_job.png`.

## Results

Size-matched at 127 qubits (noisy condition): heavy-hex-127 needs 17
two-qubit depth vs hyperbolic-127's 9, so the Eagle-style layout costs
at most **~1.9x the QPU time per shot** — an upper bound, not the
expected saving.

Sensitivity to the fixed-overhead share `s` of per-shot time
(`results/tables/cloud_cost_sensitivity.csv`, computed by the script):

| Overhead share s | Relative cost (heavy-hex / hyperbolic) |
|---|---|
| 0 (no overhead) | 1.89x |
| 0.5 | 1.44x |
| 0.9 | 1.09x |
| 0.99 (overhead dominates) | 1.01x |

Reading: the more of each shot is fixed overhead, the less geometry
moves the bill. With realistic overhead (s ≈ 0.9+), the 1.9x depth
ratio shrinks toward ~1.1x.

**Note: the first three rows below compare chips of different size
(5 / 127 / 20 qubits) — do not read them as a geometry ranking.**
The size-matched pair at the bottom is the fair comparison:

| Topology | Condition | 2q depth | Yield | Cost units |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t-shape | protected | 7 | 0.913 | 7.66 |
| heavy-hex-127 | protected | 26 | 0.795 | 32.71 |
| hyperbolic-20 | protected | 9 | 0.891 | 10.1 |
| heavy-hex-127 | noisy | 17 | 1.000 | 17.0 |
| hyperbolic-127 | noisy | 9 | 1.000 | 9.0 |

Cost units = depth / yield. Reading: on the longest route, protection
buys fidelity (+0.113) but the bill per useful shot rises, because the
deeper circuit bills longer *and* one in five shots is discarded.

## Limitations

- This is a model over simulated depths, not a measured cloud bill.
- The depth ratio is an upper bound on relative cost; measured per-shot
  overhead makes the true ratio much smaller.
- Real device gate times, parallelism, and pricing tiers vary; relative
  ordering is more robust than absolute dollars.
- Prices change; re-check before citing.
